Poetry

Rubbing

문을 열고 나오면 살아 있는 것들로 가득 차 있다Open the door, step out, and it is full of living things숨 쉬는 것들breathing things

움직이는 것들과 다르다Different from things that move죽어 있는 것도 움직일 수 있다Even dead things can move

건조하겠지It must be dry지나치게 건조하겠지It must be far too dry

건물의 벽을 만져 본다I touch the wall of a building버석하게 무언가 떨어진다Something crumbles off, brittle물기 하나 없이 검은 인주를 찍은 듯하다like a stamp of black seal ink, without a drop of moisture

오래됐네It’s old

바람에 밀려가는 쓰레기나Trash pushed along by the wind, or일정한 속도로 굴러가는 바퀴들wheels rolling at a steady speed

살아 있는 척할까Should I pretend to be alive죽어 있는 척할까Should I pretend to be dead

손에 묻은 인주를 털어낸다I brush the ink off my hand

바람이 분다The wind blows이제 걸음을 옮긴다Now I walk on

Between pretending to be alive and pretending to be dead,
poems that brush the ink off the hand and walk on

Dongwon Nam’s poems sort what breathes from what merely moves. Among building walls where something brittle crumbles off at a touch, trash pushed along by the wind and wheels rolling at a steady speed, the poet asks whether to pretend to be alive or to pretend to be dead — and, instead of answering, brushes the ink off his hand. Firm sentences that set down what he has seen, without putting feeling first, are the foundation of this collection.
